**Figure width is 5.5 in, matching `neurips.sty`'s `textwidth=5.5in`.** The class
is single-column `article`, so these are ordinary `figure` floats, not `figure*`,
and `\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]` places them **1:1** — the point sizes in
the figure are the point sizes on the page. An earlier draft authored at 7.2 in
for a two-column layout; at `\textwidth` that silently shrank everything by
0.764x, putting the heatmap's cell numbers at 5.5 pt and the `d = 1` label at
4.0 pt. Verified against the produced PDFs:

`ln -s ../figures paper/figures` or point `\graphicspath` at the real location.

## Deviations from the drawing spec, and why

**Figure 3(b): the random-direction control is MentaLLaMA's only.** The first
draft drew four per-model dashed curves; they are pure noise and buried the real
lines. The second draft pooled all four into one grey envelope — which is
unattributable, and the legend that went with it ("learned shortcut direction"
on a black line) was simply wrong, since the real curves are the four coloured
lines. The panel now shows **MentaLLaMA's own matched-random range** as a light
blue band under its own thick blue line. That is exactly the claim: MentaLLaMA
is the one model whose learned direction leaves *its own* control, over a
contiguous band of layers. The other three models' controls belong in an
appendix; each is beaten by its random direction on 15–21 of 36 layers.

**Figure 3: one shared model legend, below both panels.** Panel (b) carries no
model legend of its own, so colours had to be remembered across the figure. The
four model colours are now a single horizontal legend under both panels, and
panel (b)'s own legend names only the shaded band.

**Figure 3(b): the region annotation names the model.** "coupled region" alone
could be read as a region where *all* models are coupled; it is MentaLLaMA's,
derived from its own significant layers (8-21 of 32). The shading is also
fainter (alpha 0.04) so it stays an annotation rather than competing with the
blue control band.

**Figure 3(b): the isolated layer-1 spike is plotted but no longer annotated.**
Labelling it `(d = 0.34)` inside a partial-correlation panel invited confusion
with Panel (a)'s Cohen's *d*. The main text should instead say that the
contiguous coupled region excludes an isolated early-layer peak.

**Figure 4: the group counts sit on their own side of zero.** One revision put
them in whichever quadrant happened to be empty, which placed "4/8 improve" to
the right of zero — the *worsening* direction — and read as a mislabel even
though the bars were correct. On a diverging chart horizontal position carries
sign, so the counts now sit inside their own half, vertically centred on their
own group.

**Figure 4: no chart title, and a dagger instead of a bracket.** The
same-backbone bracket consumed about a quarter of the panel width for one
annotation; two daggers plus a footnote cost one glyph each. The title was
dropped because it collided with the directional annotations and the caption
carries the claim.

**Figure 2: symmetric colour scale, ±0.52.** An earlier version used
`TwoSlopeNorm(-0.10, 0, +0.52)` so that the negative range filled the blue half
of the map. It made −0.082 as saturated as +0.45, i.e. colour intensity was no
longer comparable across the sign. The scale is now symmetric: Irf's blues are
faint, which is faithful — those effects really are an order of magnitude
smaller than DAIC's. Exact values are printed in every cell.

**Figure 2: three decimals, not two.** At two decimals the whole mmpsy column
rendered as `0.00`, which reads as exact zero; the values are +0.005, +0.004,
+0.003, +0.002, −0.001. Only Qwen3-4B-base × mmpsy is genuinely 0.000.

## Suggested captions

**Figure 2.** *Model reliance differs substantially across statistically
available shortcuts.* Directional counterfactual shortcut sensitivity
(CSS_dir = mean paired change in predicted probability when only the
shortcut-bearing content is swapped, item content byte-identical) for eight
models on four corpora. Colour is symmetric about zero (±0.52) so
that intensity is comparable across the sign; exact values are printed in every
cell. The probe metrics beneath each column differ in task and metric across
datasets and are shown only as within-dataset
evidence that a shortcut is statistically available; they are not comparable to
one another. DAIC-WOZ moves every model; PTSD moves mainly the OmniSapiens
family; Irf is weak and frequently reversed; mmpsy is null throughout.

**Figure 3.** *Shortcut information is broadly decodable but not uniformly
behaviourally relevant.* (a) Cross-fitted linear separation between clinically
loaded and biographical interviewer contexts stays above d = 1 across 89–95 % of
layers in all four models (peaks 2.93–4.01). (b) Partial correlation between
movement along the learned shortcut direction and the per-participant prediction
shift, controlling for displacement norm, is far more selective. The shaded band
is MentaLLaMA's own matched random-direction range; its learned direction leaves
that band over a contiguous region of layers (15 of 32 layers significant, random
winning on only 5). No other model separates from its control this way — random
directions win on 15/36, 18/36 and 21/36 layers for OSim-8B, Menta and
Qwen3-4B-base respectively (appendix). Decodability and behavioural relevance are
plotted on separate axes because their dissociation is the claim.

**Figure 4.** *The same mitigation helps one model and harms another.* Change in
shortcut sensitivity after adding an instruction to ignore the interviewer, for
the eight-model DAIC sweep. Exactly four models improve and four worsen. Qwen3-4B-base
and Menta (†) share a backbone and respond in opposite directions
(0.256 → 0.058 versus 0.027 → 0.141). Instruct-ignore is shown because it is the
only arm in the shared sweep whose post-intervention CSS is measured rather than
zero by construction.
