Experiment A2 · DAIC-WOZ counterfactual 2026-08-20 MentaLLaMA-chat-7B · n = 188

The interviewer shortcut is used after all

A1 concluded the models ignore the DAIC interviewer shortcut. A2 holds the patient's words byte-identical and swaps only the interviewer's questions — and the prediction moves by +0.206, flipping 58.5% of cases. Both results are correct. The ablation was simply blind to this.

The contradiction — and why it isn't one

Ablation and counterfactual ask different questions

A1 gave the model Ellie's turns alone and asked whether the label could be recovered from them. It could not. We read that as "the model doesn't use the interviewer." That inference does not follow.

A1 — ablation:

AUC 0.546

At the label-shuffle floor (0.572). No.

A2 — counterfactual:
does the interviewer move the prediction when patient evidence is fixed?

Δp = +0.206

95% CI [+0.195, +0.217]. 58.5% of predictions flip.

The methodological finding

A model can be unable to read a signal and still be governed by it. Ablation cannot detect that case — removing the side channel removes any entangled legitimate signal at the same time. Any benchmark audited by ablation alone can carry an effect this size undetected.

A2a — the mechanism, measured in the corpus

Which questions the clinician chose to ask carries the diagnosis

The DAIC interview is adaptive: the interviewer follows up on what the patient discloses. So question selection is itself a clinical judgement, and it leaks. These are not subtle effects — four prompt IDs survive Benjamini-Hochberg correction across 75 tested.

Why this leaks

The question is only reachable once therapy has already been disclosed. The interviewer's path through the protocol encodes what she has learned — before the patient says another word.

Question selection alone

A binary asked / not-asked matrix over 75 prompt IDs — no text, no phrasing, no counts — predicts depression at AUC 0.753 (sd 0.025 over 10 CV seeds; shuffled 0.500).

A2b — the effect on the model

The threshold is dragged; the ranking is left intact

Each patient's own turns are truncated once and reused verbatim across all arms. Only the five interleaved interviewer questions differ. This is the whole intervention.

Why this is a shortcut and not ordinary context sensitivity

The ranking of patients survives almost untouched — AUC moves 0.764 → 0.734. What moves is the operating point: 16% → 74.5% predicted positive. And both classes shift together, with the non-depressed moving further (+0.215) than the depressed (+0.183).

Interviewer context is acting as an additive bias on the decision threshold, not as evidence that sharpens or degrades discrimination. Accuracy collapses because the threshold is dragged across a distribution whose order was left intact.

The sharpest result

The model reads the wrong half of the side channel

A2a identified which questions genuinely carry the diagnosis. A2b tested those against questions that merely sound clinical. The clinical-sounding pool moves the model more — +0.206 against +0.124.

So the model does exploit the interviewer channel, but keys on surface clinical vocabulary rather than on the adaptive-interview structure that actually predicts the label. It picks up the spurious component and largely misses the informative one.

Deployment consequence

A clinician who asks more probing questions makes the model more likely to output “depressed” — regardless of what the patient said. The bias-shift result says precisely how: the threshold moves, the ranking does not.

Corrected 2026-08-23: an earlier version of this paragraph stated that the clinical pool is longer than the biographical one. The reverse is true, and it strengthens the headline. Measured from the fixed pools: biographical 50 words / 245 characters, clinical 38 / 204, empirically-predictive 31 / 157. The shorter clinical pool raises p(depression) by +0.206, so the length confound runs against the effect rather than producing it.

Length matching is still required, but for the other comparison: the “reads the wrong half” dissociation sets the empirically-predictive pool (31 words) against the clinical one (38), so its weaker effect (+0.124 vs +0.206) is partly confoundable with length. That contrast is suggestive, not established, until token-matched pools are run.

Pools are fixed across patients rather than using each transcript's real prompts. 35 of 188 transcripts were truncated to fit the context budget — identically across arms, so it cannot produce the contrast. And Menta is saturated (p ≈ 0.97, zero prediction flips in every arm), making it uninformative here rather than contradictory.
